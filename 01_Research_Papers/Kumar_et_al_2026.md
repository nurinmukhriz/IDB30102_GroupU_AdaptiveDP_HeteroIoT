# Kumar et al. (2026)

## Paper Title
Trust and Transparency in IoT: The Role of Federated Learning and Blockchain

## Author(s)
Rohit Kumar, U. Padmavathi, and Neha Agrawal

## Year
2026

## Research Problem
The study examines security, privacy, trust, and transparency challenges in Internet of Things (IoT) environments. The large number of heterogeneous IoT devices and the sensitive data they generate create risks such as data breaches, privacy violations, and lack of trust in data exchanges.

## Method / Technique
This paper presents a survey of Federated Learning (FL) and Blockchain technologies for IoT. Federated Learning is discussed as a privacy-preserving approach that allows devices to collaboratively train machine learning models without sharing raw data. Blockchain is examined as a mechanism for improving transparency, data integrity, traceability, and trust.

## Dataset / Tools
This is a survey/review paper and does not propose a specific experimental dataset or implementation tool.

## Main Findings
The study highlights that Federated Learning can improve privacy by keeping raw IoT data on local devices, while Blockchain can provide transparent, verifiable, and tamper-resistant records. The combination of FL and Blockchain can strengthen privacy, security, trust, and transparency in IoT systems.

## Limitation
The integration of Federated Learning and Blockchain may introduce scalability and computational overhead. The paper also highlights challenges related to regulatory requirements and large-scale real-world deployment.

## Relevance to Proposed Research
This paper is relevant to the proposed research because it highlights privacy and heterogeneity challenges in IoT environments and discusses Federated Learning as a privacy-preserving approach. It supports the need for privacy mechanisms that can be efficiently applied in heterogeneous IoT environments.
